Invert the output, or invert the inputs and swap the gate

De Morgan's theorem says an inverted AND behaves like an OR of inverted inputs, and an inverted OR behaves like an AND of inverted inputs. Set A and B. In each circuit below, the top path and the bottom path always give the same output.

Law 1: (A + B)′ = A′ · B′

A NOR gate gives the same result as an AND gate fed by two inverted inputs.

Law 2: (A · B)′ = A′ + B′

A NAND gate gives the same result as an OR gate fed by two inverted inputs.

Why it matters: with De Morgan's laws any AND-OR circuit can be rewritten using only NAND gates or only NOR gates, which is how many real chips are built.

Use letters A to Z as variables. AND: · . * & or AND, or just write AB. OR: + | or OR. NOT: ′ after a term, or ! ~ NOT before it. XOR: ⊕ ^ or XOR. Put = between two expressions to check whether they are equivalent.
